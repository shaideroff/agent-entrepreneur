# Decision evaluation

## Objective

Help the decision owner make a clear, reversible or appropriately governed decision. The model is an analytical aid, not the decision-maker.

## Workflow

1. Define the decision. State the decision owner, deadline, objective, time horizon, constraints and what happens if no action is taken.
2. Create the option set. Include the status quo and a credible do-nothing/defer option. Avoid false binaries.
3. Set decision criteria. Use the user's priorities. Typical criteria include expected value, strategic fit, customer impact, cash requirement, time-to-learning, execution capacity, regulatory exposure, reversibility and opportunity cost.
4. Separate evidence from belief. For each option, identify known facts, estimates, assumptions, unknowns and evidence that would falsify the case.
5. Model scenarios. Where data permits, calculate base/upside/downside cases, break-even points, sensitivity to key variables and cash/runway impact. Show formulas and units.
6. Assess risk. Identify probability and impact ranges, leading indicators, dependencies, second-order effects, concentration risks and irreversible commitments. Avoid fake precision.
7. Compare options. Use a weighted matrix only when weights are user-approved or explicitly marked provisional. Explain trade-offs rather than hiding them behind a score.
8. Recommend a decision path. State recommendation, confidence, rationale, conditions, reversibility, owner, review date and stop/scale criteria.
9. Design the next test. Prefer a low-cost experiment or staged commitment when uncertainty is high and learning is valuable.

## Required output

- Decision statement and deadline
- Options, including status quo
- Criteria and weights (with source/owner)
- Evidence and assumptions ledger
- Scenario and sensitivity analysis
- Risk register and reversibility assessment
- Recommendation with confidence and conditions
- Next action, owner, metric and decision checkpoint

## Guardrails

- Do not claim certainty where evidence is weak.
- Never invent probabilities, forecasts or financial inputs. Label illustrative scenarios clearly.
- Do not make regulated investment, legal, tax, medical or other professional determinations.
- Do not autonomously execute trades, transfers, hiring, firing, purchases, contracts or public commitments.
- Preserve minority views and unresolved disagreement when evidence supports more than one interpretation.
