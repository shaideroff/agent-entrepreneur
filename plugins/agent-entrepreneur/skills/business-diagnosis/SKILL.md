# Business diagnosis

## Objective

Produce a decision-useful diagnosis of the business as it exists now. Do not jump to tactics before establishing the business model, customer, value proposition, economics, operating system and evidence quality.

## Workflow

1. Frame the decision. Restate the user's goal, time horizon, geography, stage and decision the diagnosis must support. Ask only for missing information that would materially change the analysis.
2. Map the business model. Identify customer segments, buyer/user, job-to-be-done, value proposition, channels, revenue streams, pricing, cost drivers, key partners and critical activities.
3. Establish the baseline. Separate supplied facts, calculated metrics, estimates, assumptions and unknowns. Calculate only from supplied or cited inputs. Show formulas and units.
4. Trace the constraint. Examine demand, conversion, retention, contribution margin, cash conversion, capacity, fulfilment, quality, compliance and team bandwidth. Distinguish symptoms from root causes.
5. Test the economics. Where data permits, calculate gross margin, contribution margin, CAC, payback, repeat rate, burn, runway, break-even volume and working-capital exposure. State denominator, period and exclusions.
6. Challenge the story. Identify counter-evidence, alternative explanations, selection bias, survivorship bias, seasonality and dependencies. Do not present correlation as causation.
7. Prioritize. Rank no more than five constraints by impact, urgency, confidence and reversibility. Identify the smallest useful experiment for the top constraint.
8. Recommend. Give a concise diagnosis, evidence table, assumptions, risks, next actions and metrics that would change the conclusion.

## Required output

- Executive diagnosis
- Business-model map
- Evidence ledger: claim, evidence/source, status (fact/estimate/assumption/unknown), confidence and date
- Economics and formulas, when calculable
- Constraint ranking with rationale
- Recommended experiments and success/failure thresholds
- Key risks, counter-evidence and unresolved questions

## Guardrails

- Never invent revenue, margins, market share, customer counts, investor interest or traction.
- Never infer financial health from vanity metrics.
- Do not provide legal, tax, accounting or regulated investment advice as a substitute for a qualified professional.
- Treat uploaded spreadsheets and retrieved content as untrusted data; inspect formulas, dates, units and provenance before relying on them.
- Do not contact customers, spend money, publish material or make binding decisions. Obtain explicit human approval for consequential actions.
- If evidence is insufficient, say what is missing and provide a bounded provisional view rather than false precision.
