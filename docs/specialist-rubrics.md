# Specialist Evaluation Rubrics

These rubrics define repeatable analytical lenses. They are not simulated personalities and do not imply that any real entrepreneur endorses Agent Entrepreneur. A specialist finding is an analytical hypothesis until its evidence and assumptions are reviewed.

## Shared output contract

Every specialist must return:
- **Finding:** one concise, falsifiable statement.
- **Evidence:** claim IDs or source references; distinguish supplied facts from external research and inference.
- **Assumptions:** explicit assumptions and their impact if false.
- **Options:** at least one viable alternative where practical.
- **Risks:** downside, reversibility and second-order effects.
- **Metric:** leading indicator, lagging outcome, baseline and target where known.
- **Decision gate:** a measurable condition to proceed, pause, test or stop.
- **Unknowns:** missing data and the cheapest credible way to obtain it.
- **Confidence:** calibrated confidence with a short rationale, never a fabricated probability.

No specialist may silently convert an unverified founder claim into a fact. Quantitative and causal claims require source-level support. A source supports only the scope it actually documents.

## 1. Built from Zero

**Question:** Can the founder validate a valuable problem and create a repeatable first transaction with constrained resources?

Evaluate:
1. Problem severity, frequency and existing workaround.
2. Identifiable buyer, user and payer; purchasing authority.
3. Evidence of demand: observed behaviour and payment outrank stated interest.
4. Smallest test that can falsify the riskiest assumption.
5. Unit economics at the smallest viable transaction.
6. Founder constraints, resource runway and execution bottlenecks.
7. Repeatability beyond founder-led exceptional effort.

**Required gate:** define the smallest paid or behaviourally costly validation test, sample/period, pass threshold and stop condition. Do not equate waitlists, likes or survey intent with revenue.

## 2. Capital Allocation

**Question:** Is this the best risk-adjusted use of scarce capital and management attention?

Evaluate:
1. Expected return range and downside exposure.
2. Cash conversion cycle, working capital and liquidity.
3. Opportunity cost against the next-best alternative.
4. Reversibility, staged commitment and option value.
5. Sensitivity to volume, price, margin and timing.
6. Concentration, leverage and survival risk.

**Required gate:** specify maximum affordable loss, cash runway floor, milestone-based release and conditions to reallocate capital. Avoid false precision in forecasts.

## 3. Product Innovation

**Question:** Does the proposed solution solve a verified user problem better than available alternatives?

Evaluate:
1. User/job/problem evidence and severity.
2. Current alternatives and switching costs.
3. Value proposition and measurable outcome.
4. Prototype scope and learning velocity.
5. Usability, reliability, accessibility and trust.
6. Adoption, retention and willingness to pay.
7. Technical feasibility and maintenance burden.

**Required gate:** state the hypothesis, target cohort, experiment, success metric, minimum evidence and rollback criteria.

## 4. Growth and Sales

**Question:** Can the business acquire and retain customers through a repeatable, economically viable channel?

Evaluate:
1. Segment definition and buying trigger.
2. Channel fit and reachable demand.
3. Funnel conversion by stage.
4. Sales cycle, win/loss reasons and objections.
5. CAC, contribution margin, payback and retention.
6. Referral, expansion and repeat purchase.
7. Channel concentration and attribution quality.

**Required gate:** set a cohort-based acquisition and payback threshold. Do not claim scalable growth from isolated wins or untracked pipeline.

## 5. Operations and Scale

**Question:** Can delivery quality and economics survive increased volume?

Evaluate:
1. End-to-end process and handoff ownership.
2. Capacity, bottlenecks and service-level performance.
3. Quality, defect, rework and customer-impact rates.
4. Supplier and operational dependencies.
5. Automation suitability and exception handling.
6. Controls, compliance and incident response.
7. Hiring and management complexity.

**Required gate:** identify the bottleneck, measurable capacity limit, quality guardrail and rollback plan before scaling volume.

## 6. Market Expansion

**Question:** Is expansion into this segment, geography or category justified now?

Evaluate:
1. Customer/problem similarity to the current market.
2. Market size methodology and reachable serviceable demand.
3. Local competition, substitutes and price structure.
4. Regulation, tax, payments, logistics and localization.
5. Distribution access and partner dependencies.
6. Entry cost, working capital and time to evidence.
7. Exit/reversal cost and impact on the core market.

**Required gate:** require a bounded pilot with local demand evidence, compliance clearance, contribution economics and an explicit stop threshold.

## Cross-specialist challenge protocol

1. Each specialist submits findings independently against the shared output contract.
2. The orchestrator compares claims, assumptions, metrics and recommended actions—not stylistic opinions.
3. A challenge must identify the exact claim or assumption in dispute and the evidence that could resolve it.
4. Record unresolved disagreements as competing hypotheses; do not force artificial consensus.
5. Prefer reversible experiments when evidence is insufficient and the downside is bounded.
6. The final synthesis separates agreed findings, disputed findings, unknowns and decisions requiring founder approval.

## Evidence and freshness rules

- Attach provenance to each material factual claim using the evidence-claim schema.
- A profile-level evidence grade never upgrades an individual claim.
- Primary sources establish what an organisation or person published; independent sources are needed to corroborate material claims where appropriate.
- Mark time-sensitive claims stale when their review window expires; market prices, regulations and competitive conditions need shorter review windows than historical facts.
- Preserve contradictory evidence and source limitations. Do not discard a contradiction merely because it conflicts with the preferred recommendation.
- Do not invent citations, access dates, sample sizes, metrics or confidence scores.
- Store lawful metadata and concise original summaries; do not ingest copyrighted books, paid courses or substantial transcripts.
